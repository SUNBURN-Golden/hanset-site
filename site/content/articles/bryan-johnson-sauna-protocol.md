# Bryan Johnson's Sauna Protocol, Read Closely

Hot room, ice on the face. Here is what he says, and what the research says.

## 본문
On October 21, longevity entrepreneur Bryan Johnson posted a 94-second video of his sauna routine on X. His own verdict: "my sauna protocol is pretty ridiculous."

### The protocol, in his words

Johnson lists a 200°F (93°C) sauna, 20 minutes a session, five sessions a week. He sits on what he describes as medical-grade silicone, with ice on his face, his neck, his groin, and his continuous glucose monitor.

He offers one reason, and labels it carefully: cooling the face and neck "is a hypothesis of protecting from heat induced collagen breakdown." His post cites no study.

### What the research says

Frequency. A Finnish cohort of 2,315 middle-aged men, followed for about 20 years, found that men who used a sauna four to seven times a week had lower rates of fatal cardiovascular events and all-cause death than once-a-week users (1). It is an observational study of Finnish men. It shows an association, not cause.

Ice on the groin. Heat affects sperm. In an Italian study, regular Finnish sauna sessions caused a significant but reversible drop in sperm quality in healthy men (2). Cooling the area follows that logic. Whether ice prevents the effect has not been tested.

Ice on the face. A review of human skin studies reports that heat and infrared exposure induce matrix metalloproteinases, enzymes that break down collagen, and may add to skin aging (3). That supports the question behind his idea. It does not show that ice on the face during a sauna protects the skin.

### In Seoul

Korea's bathhouse culture runs on the same hot-cold rhythm: a dry room, then a cold plunge. Boutique saunas and hotel saunas across Seoul now offer the same contrast in quieter rooms. Johnson's routine is one person's experiment, not a standard to copy.
